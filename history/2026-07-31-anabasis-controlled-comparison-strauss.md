## Inputs

The comparison uses two separately owner-adopted evidence streams:

1. `XEN-PRIMARY-CUMULATIVE-001`, derived from all fifty-one chapter reconstructions of the admitted H. G. Dakyns Project Gutenberg English translation witness of Xenophon's *Anabasis*.
2. `XEN-STUDY-SEC-001`, the eight-unit reconstruction of Leo Strauss's essay "Xenophon's Anabasis," printed on pages 105-136 of *Studies in Platonic Political Philosophy*.

Neither stream was rewritten. The comparison is additive and preserves their independent jurisdictions.

## Principal agreements

The strongest agreements concern:

- the displacement from Cyrus's concealed kingship project to the Greeks' survival and political self-organization;
- Xenophon's ascent into practical authority without reduction to one formal office;
- audience-differentiated speech;
- speech tested by deed, failure, correction, and outcome;
- piety intertwined with prudence;
- signs and sacrifices as non-self-interpreting;
- practical effectiveness as distinct from justice;
- the army as an intermittent mobile political community;
- the political and material complications of homecoming;
- the recurrent use of the army by external powers that resist assuming all its burdens.

## Qualified agreements and divergences

Primary evidence supports Strauss's attention to founding, monarchy, fatherland, justice, and narrator problems, but it does not independently establish:

- Themistogenes as Xenophon's pseudonym;
- praise-by-omission or `legetai` as verified Greek mechanisms;
- Socrates as the governing standard of the *Anabasis*;
- Book Four's numerical and compositional centrality;
- a final hierarchy between manly and Socratic justice;
- a settled ranking of Greece, Athens, Sparta, Cyrus, and fatherland.

The sharpest divergence concerns the ending. The primary cumulative reconstruction foregrounds the army's institutional transfer to Thibron and the absence of narrated homecoming. Strauss foregrounds reconciliation, Greece over barbarians, the unresolved Socratic apprehension, renewed Persian war, and the density of piety and oaths in Book Seven.

## Preserved limits

Agreement is not certification. Divergence is not error. Silence is not refutation.

The comparison does not authenticate the Greek text, adjudicate translations, certify narrator identity, verify numerical patterns, resolve divine causation, establish a final teaching, derive a deed corpus or speech register, create an operational capacity, derive a minister adapter, register Xenophon in Sanctum, or authorize Assembly dispatch.
