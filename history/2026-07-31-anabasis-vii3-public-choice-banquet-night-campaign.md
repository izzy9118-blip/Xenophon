## Documentary movement

1. The delegates return before dawn. The leaders refuse another summons from Aristarchus and convene the army without Neon's detached men.
2. Xenophon discloses Aristarchus's and Seuthes's alternatives, but moves that the soldiers first enter country where they can provision themselves. All hands approve.
3. The army ignores attempts by Neon and Aristarchus's agents to stop it. Xenophon asks Seuthes publicly and before many witnesses to guide it to provisions.
4. Seuthes leads the army to closely packed villages, states pay, provisioning, capture, honor, and distance terms, and receives an unopposed vote.
5. Heracleides solicits gifts before the banquet. Guests present animals, persons, clothing, silver, and textiles; Arystas's refusal to distribute food makes the exchange comic as well as political.
6. Xenophon, nearly penniless, offers himself and his companions as willing friends and promises acquisitions, God willing. Seuthes accepts fraternally.
7. The Greeks arrange night sentinels and restrict Thracian entry. Seuthes proposes immediate surprise; Xenophon advises that the slowest arm lead the march.
8. Athenaia is chosen as watchword. Heavy infantry leads at midnight, light infantry follows, and cavalry guards the rear.
9. Deep snow preserves surprise. Xenophon dismounts and advances with younger soldiers, Cleanor brings the rest, and the force occupies the heights.
10. The first operation yields one thousand captives, two thousand cattle, and ten thousand smaller animals.

## Preserved distinctions

- Public disclosure of alternatives is distinct from private self-dealing.
- Moving first for provisions is distinct from the final decision to serve Seuthes.
- A unanimous vote establishes procedure but does not prove that every promise is just or reliable.
- Seuthes's assignment of captured property to payroll remains distinct from verified accounting and payment.
- Heracleides's solicitations are documented without certifying either custom or corruption.
- Xenophon's poverty explains his nonmaterial gift without certifying his entire character.
- Banquet generosity and comedy are not treated as conclusive character evidence.
- Xenophon's offer of friendship does not transfer constitutional sovereignty over the army.
- “God willing” is preserved as attributed sacred language, not transparent proof of divine causation.
- Claimed Thracian-Athenian kinship does not become established history merely through the watchword.
- Tactical success does not resolve the justice of conquest, captivity, or enslavement.
- Translator wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence; secondary comparison remains deferred.
