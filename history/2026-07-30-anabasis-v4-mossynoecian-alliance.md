# Anabasis V.4 Mossynoecian Alliance and Conquest Draft Record

Date: 2026-07-30

`XEN-PRI-RU-033` covers *Anabasis* V.4 on one-based PDF pages 101-103 and remains `DRAFTED_PENDING_OWNER_REVIEW`. Editorial note (5) from V.3 remains paratext above heading IV; V.5 begins beneath heading V on page 104 and ends before heading VI on page 107.

Timesitheus, a Trapezuntine proxenos of the Mossynoecians, mediates after one faction refuses passage. He identifies a rival faction and interprets Xenophon’s alliance proposal, which joins Greek safe passage to local vengeance, future subjection of the opponents, and reciprocal military aid. Pledges are exchanged, and three hundred single-trunk canoes deliver allied fighters.

The allies advance singing toward a fortress. Some Greeks join without orders for plunder, and an enemy sortie routs them. The victors decapitate dead opponents and display the heads while dancing and singing. Xenophon answers with an assembly speech that treats the defeat as proof of the alliance’s authenticity and as punishment for contempt of discipline.

After favorable sacrifice, the Greeks and their allies advance in columns with coordinated missile support. The defenders abandon the fortress; the king and others remain in wooden towers and are burned. The Greeks pillage food stores, eat, transfer the stronghold to their allies, and continue through settlements that are deserted or change sides.

The chapter closes with an ethnographic description of Mossynoecian bodies, elite child-feeding, tattooing, sexuality, and public-private conduct, followed by a superlative judgment that they are the most barbaric and outlandish people encountered. The unit preserves this as culturally situated Greek evaluation, not neutral anthropology.

Books I through IV have complete sequential draft coverage pending owner review. Book V now has drafted coverage through V.4. The next unit is `XEN-PRI-RU-034`, covering V.5 on PDF pages 104-106.
