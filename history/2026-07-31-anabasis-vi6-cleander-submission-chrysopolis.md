## Documentary movement

1. Enemy households and property are withdrawn; the Greeks provision themselves daily from the surrounding country under a public/private booty rule.
2. Markets arrive from nearby Greek cities, while rumor of a city and harbor being founded affects merchants, surrounding communities, and enemy envoys.
3. Cleander arrives with two warships but no transports.
4. Men returning with sheep privately offer Dexippus a share; he seizes a protesting soldier, Agasias rescues the man, and soldiers stone Dexippus.
5. Cleander threatens to declare the army public enemies and exclude it from Hellenic cities.
6. Xenophon argues that Spartan command on land and sea requires submission, offers himself for judgment and the extreme penalty if guilty, and advises the army not to shield offenders.
7. Agasias denies that Xenophon ordered the rescue and voluntarily surrenders. Cleander receives the accused under guard.
8. Agasias defends his intervention; Cleander states that even a scamp is entitled to a fair trial. The rescued soldier invokes the ordinance making the sheep public property.
9. Xenophon intercedes for release. Cleander frees the men, associates himself with Xenophon, and proposes leading the army if the gods permit.
10. Sacrifices remain adverse for three successive days. Cleander accepts and then returns the public cattle gift and departs.
11. The soldiers divide corn and captured property, march through Bithynia, reverse direction for one day and night to obtain slaves and small cattle, reach Chrysopolis on the sixth day, and spend seven days selling booty.

## Preserved distinctions

- Commercial and diplomatic effects of city-founding rumor are not treated as constitutional founding.
- Public booty rules remain distinct from private appropriation and from the justice of each intervention.
- Dexippus's alleged or demonstrated character does not remove the accused person's claim to trial.
- Spartan military supremacy is recorded as Xenophon's premise for submission, not certified as just title.
- Voluntary submission to jurisdiction is distinct from passivity or loss of political agency.
- Cleander's willingness to lead remains distinct from divine authorization and actual command.
- Adverse sacrifices are narrated constraints, not demonstrated transparent divine causation.
- A homeward march remains compatible with continued raiding and acquisition.
- Arrival at Chrysopolis is not yet completed return to individual homes in Hellas.
- Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence, and later comparison remains deferred.
