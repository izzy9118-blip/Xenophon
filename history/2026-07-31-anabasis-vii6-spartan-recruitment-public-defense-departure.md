## Documentary movement

Charminus and Polynicus arrive from Thibron with a quantified Spartan offer. Heracleides treats their arrival as a means for Seuthes to dispose of the army without satisfying its pay claim. Seuthes offers the troops, seeks Lacedaemonian friendship, and excludes Xenophon and the other generals from the magnificent hospitality.

At the public assembly an unnamed Arcadian accuses Xenophon of private enrichment and asks that he be stoned. Xenophon answers through the army's earlier vote, his initial refusal of Seuthes, the benefits and dangers of the Thracian alliance, his own losses, and a formal oath that he received none of Seuthes's private promises. Charminus repeats Seuthes's testimony that Xenophon's defect was excessive friendship toward the soldiers.

Eurylochus asks the Spartans to exact full pay. Polycrates accuses Heracleides of retaining the proceeds of captured property. Heracleides and Seuthes withdraw rapidly. Seuthes then attempts to retain Xenophon with one thousand hoplites, renewed coastal promises, and a warning that Thibron will kill him. Xenophon sacrifices two victims to Zeus the King; the reported answer is to depart.
