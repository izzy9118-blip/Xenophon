## Documentary movement

1. Pharnabazus fears an attack on his satrapy and works through Anaxibius to remove the army from Asia.
2. Anaxibius promises pay if the soldiers cross to Byzantium.
3. Xenophon delays his intended departure but refuses to make the already planned crossing a private service to Seuthes.
4. After the crossing, Anaxibius withholds pay and expels the army without provisions.
5. The soldiers force reentry by three routes and cause panic in Byzantium.
6. They offer Xenophon the conjunction of city, triremes, money, men, and greatness.
7. Xenophon uses apparent assent to restore formation, convenes an assembly, and argues against occupation and pillage.
8. He distinguishes the wrongdoing of particular Spartans from innocent Byzantium and warns that seizure would bring war with all Hellas.
9. The army chooses disciplined negotiation, accepts Coeratadas, and withdraws.
10. Anaxibius praises discipline but threatens to sell any soldier found inside.
11. Coeratadas cannot secure favorable sacrifice or supply even one day of provisions and abandons generalship.

## Controlled distinctions

- promised pay is not paid compensation;
- valid grievance is not authority to punish an innocent city;
- forced entry is not legitimate acquisition;
- military self-formation is not a constitutional transfer of sovereignty;
- tactical apparent assent is not completed acceptance of rule;
- refusal of Byzantium is strong evidence of restraint but not certification of complete justice;
- Spartan predominance is not demonstrated Spartan justice;
- Hellenic loyalty is not identical with established Athenian loyalty;
- sacrificial adversity is not transparent divine causation;
- title to command is not logistical capacity;
- narrator report, attributed speech, motive report, deed, and outcome remain distinct.
