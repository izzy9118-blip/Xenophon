Date: 2026-07-30

The generals initially seek to attack weaker Tibarenian coastal fortresses for material gain, but repeated sacrifice culminates in a seerly judgment that the gods do not countenance war. The Greeks accept hospitality and proceed through territory now treated as friendly.

At Cotyora a forty-five-day halt combines sacrifices, tribal processions, games, provisioning from Paphlagonia and Cotyorite estates, and conflict over market access and shelter for the sick. A Sinopean embassy led by Hecatonymus protests forcible entry, quartering, and seizure, invokes colonial and tributary claims, and threatens alliance with Paphlagonians.

Xenophon replies that paid markets produce reciprocal restraint, whereas refusal of markets forces provisioning from necessity rather than insolence. He defends controlled entry for sick and wounded, payment of expenses, a sentry at the gates, and the main army's encampment outside, while answering the Sinopean threat with a counter-threat of Paphlagonian alliance.

Other ambassadors distance themselves from Hecatonymus's tone, affirm friendship, and promise hospitality and instructions to Cotyora. Gifts, entertainment, and route consultation end the chapter without adjudicating the underlying factual or legal dispute. The Cotyorites' direct voice remains absent.
