## Documentary movement

1. The army moves into Calpe's natural fastness, digs a ditch, erects a palisade, and leaves three gates.
2. A ship from Heraclea arrives with barleymeal, wine, and sacrificial animals.
3. Xenophon's first victim is favorable; Arexion sees an eagle, interprets it favorably, and urges him to lead.
4. Neon is initially left to guard camp, but shame draws most of his men into the expedition, leaving only men above forty-five.
5. The army advances in formation while systematically recovering and burying the dead from Neon's failed provisioning expedition.
6. Provisions are seized under the cover of formation without dispersal into the villages.
7. Spithridates and Rhathines appear with cavalry and infantry. A second sacrifice is favorable at the first attempt.
8. Xenophon proposes three reserve brigades of about two hundred men each under Samolas, Pyrrhias, and Phrasias.
9. Before a wooded gully, Xenophon argues that attack is less dangerous than retreat and that difficult ground behind the army can reinforce the necessity of victory.
10. The Greeks cross broadly, invoke Zeus the Saviour and Heracles the Guide, attack, pursue the enemy wings and cavalry, erect a trophy, and return at sunset.

## Preserved distinctions

- Military occupation and fortification of Calpe are not treated as consent to found a city.
- The arrival of supplies is not treated as proof of divine causation.
- Arexion's interpretation of the eagle remains an attributed expert judgment.
- Shame-driven participation is not treated as identical with sound allocation of guard duty.
- Ordered provisioning under formation remains distinct from the dispersed foraging disaster of VI.4.
- Reserve troops are not treated as inactive merely because they are initially uncommitted.
- Xenophon's confidence before the gully is preserved as tactical argument and encouragement, not certified foreknowledge.
- Favorable signs, logistics, formation, speeches, initiative, premature light-troop action, and enemy withdrawal remain distinct causal elements.
- The trophy records a public victory claim but not permanent pacification.
- Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence.
- Later secondary comparison remains deferred.
