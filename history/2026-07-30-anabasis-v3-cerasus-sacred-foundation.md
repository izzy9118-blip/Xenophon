Cheirisophus has not returned, ships are insufficient, and further provisioning is impossible. The army therefore divides its movement: the sick, persons over forty, boys, women, and dispensable baggage embark under Philesius and Sophaenetus, while the remainder march over the completed road to Cerasus.

At Cerasus an armed review counts 8,600 survivors. The missing are attributed collectively to enemy action, snow, and disease. Money from captives sold is divided, and a tithe for Apollo and Artemis is apportioned among the generals for divine custody. The unit preserves the absent identities and voices of the captives and does not convert sacred allocation into moral vindication.

Xenophon uses his Apollo portion for a Delphi offering inscribed with his own name and Proxenus's. His Artemis portion is deposited with Megabyzus under conditional instructions before a dangerous later campaign. After restoration during Xenophon's exile at Scillus, the fund becomes oracle-indicated land, an altar, temple, annual tithe, communal festival, hunting practice, and a sacred-property inscription.

The chapter is an extended retrospective autobiographical excursus narrated in the third person. It supplies intimate information about Xenophon's later life and a named inscription but contains no unframed first-person narrator identification. Narrator-author identity, chronology, inscription text, oracle, estate labor, succession, and historical verification remain unresolved.
