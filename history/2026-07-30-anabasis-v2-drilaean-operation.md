# Anabasis V.2 Drilaean Operation Draft Record

Date: 2026-07-30

`XEN-PRI-RU-031` covers *Anabasis* V.2 on one-based PDF pages 96-98 and remains `DRAFTED_PENDING_OWNER_REVIEW`.

When one-day provisioning becomes impossible, Xenophon takes Trapezuntine guides and half the army against the Drilae while leaving half to protect the camp. The guides' local friendships and grievances shape the target: they avoid friendly neighboring peoples but willingly lead against Drilae who have harmed them.

Drilae burn easily captured positions and withdraw to a fortified metropolis. Greek light troops and armed foragers rush across a ravine and become trapped. Xenophon and the officers weigh withdrawal against reinforcement; expected retreat losses, confidence that the place can be taken, and a seers' report of battle with a good outcome lead to commitment of the heavy infantry.

A coordinated assault combines captain-level formation, readied missiles, a crescent disposition, hymn, clarion, war cry, heavy-infantry charge, and firebrands. Agasias and Philoxenus scale the outer works after removing heavy armor. Light troops begin looting, but an inner citadel counterattack reverses the apparent capture. Tolmides then proclaims entry to all who wish to capture anything, and the incoming crowd drives the defenders back into the citadel.

After the citadel is judged impregnable, the Greeks prepare a staged retreat. An unexplained house fire is narrated as divine assistance. Xenophon treats it as a lesson of fortune and orders a broader fire barrier. The city is burned except for the citadel, enabling the army to escape.

On the next day Mysus and ten Cretans stage a false ambush to cover the army's descent with provisions. Mysus is wounded and rescued under missile fire while Cretan archers provide cover; all return to camp. The note explaining Mysus as both a Mysian and a personal name remains witness paratext.

The unit preserves guide partiality, sacrificial prediction, vanguard overextension, competitive excellence, tactical looting, corrected perceptions, divine and fortune language, urban destruction, deception, rescue, and absent Drilaean voices without importing Strauss or treating Dakyns's English as unmediated Greek evidence.
