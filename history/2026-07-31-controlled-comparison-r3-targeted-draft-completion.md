## Controlled ambiguity

R3 distinguishes the two Cyruses without separating them into unrelated compartments:

- the younger Cyrus supplies the title, expedition, Proxenos-mediated attraction, friendship, ambition, wealth, foreign service, and fatherland problem;
- the older Cyrus supplies Strauss's explicit comparative standard of inherited legitimacy, knowledge of rule, cruelty, manly virtue, and justice;
- the Cyrus-side ambiguity names Strauss's deliberate movement from the first role to the second.

The ambiguity of the two Cyrus-functions is governed by Strauss's explicit statement that justice is ambiguous and by his placement of Xenophon between the older Cyrus and Socrates.

## Byzantium

A new deep examination treats Byzantium as the concrete conjunction of foundation and monarchy. Xenophon is offered city, triremes, money, and army, yet refuses to plunder a wholly innocent Greek city and seize unauthorized rule. The refusal is stronger evidence than a verbal denial but does not certify pure motive or complete justice.
