## Witness boundary

The unit reconstructs *Anabasis* VI.2 from the admitted H. G. Dakyns Project Gutenberg translation, one-based PDF pages 122–124. It begins beneath heading II with the departure from Harmene and ends before heading III, after Xenophon lands on the Thracian frontier and advances inland.

The Jason-and-rivers itinerary, Heraclea and Cerberus notes, Calpe note, force-total note, and manuscript-summary note remain identified as translator or editorial paratext. Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence.

## Documentary movement

The army reaches the friendly Greek colony of Heraclea and receives hospitality gifts. Scarcity nevertheless produces a proposal to demand money. Cheirisophus and Xenophon refuse to compel a friendly Hellenic city to give involuntarily. The substitute embassy leads Heraclea to secure its goods, market, gates, and walls.

Arcadians and Achaeans then blame the generals, invoke their numerical majority and military contribution, secede, and elect ten generals governed by majority decision. Cheirisophus’s sole authority ends within less than a week. Neon urges separate action in expectation of Spartan vessels, while the narrator attributes to him an exclusive passage-home motive. Xenophon wishes to depart but, after sacrificing to Heracles the Leader, remains responsible for the soldiers faithful to him.

The army divides into three materially unequal bodies and takes three routes toward Calpe. The chapter ends before those rival arrangements are tested.

## Preserved distinctions

- Hospitality is not identical with tribute or compelled payment.
- A genuine provisioning need does not itself establish lawful jurisdiction over a friendly city.
- The narrator confirms the Arcadian and Achaean numerical premise without certifying their complete claim to merit.
- Majority, Spartan authority, and attachment to Xenophon become distinct bases of command.
- Xenophon’s reported sacrifice restrains his private wish to leave; the mode of divine indication remains unresolved.
- No outcome in VI.2 alone certifies the superiority or justice of any of the three divisions.
