## Documentary movement

1. The Arcadian-Achaean force divides among nearby villages for a surprise raid and appoints a knoll as rendezvous.
2. Initial capture of people and cattle is followed by Thracian regrouping, destruction of Smicres's company, and near destruction of Hegesander's.
3. The surviving heavy infantry is surrounded by cavalry and missile troops, wounded without effective retaliation, cut off from water, and driven to an incomplete truce negotiation.
4. Cheirisophus reaches Calpe safely. Xenophon's cavalry acquires intelligence from old men, whom Xenophon keeps guarded as possible guides.
5. Xenophon argues that the Arcadians' destruction would endanger every separated division and orders a rescue march using cavalry reconnaissance, flank scouts, fire, watchfires, prayer, battle order, and speed.
6. The Thracians withdraw after sundown; the Arcadians depart at dawn; Xenophon's force follows their track to Calpe.
7. The reunited divisions rejoice and embrace like brothers. The Arcadians explain that the extinguished fires first signaled an expected rescue attack and later appeared to signal Xenophontic flight.

## Preserved distinctions

- Initial booty success is not treated as strategic success.
- Heavy infantry strength is not treated as universal tactical superiority.
- Negotiated concessions are not treated as a completed truce where hostages remain unresolved.
- Xenophon's reported belief and public argument remain distinct from narrator certification.
- The possible divine explanation is preserved as Xenophon's qualified speech, not established causation.
- Fire as intended military communication remains distinct from the interpretations others place upon it.
- Fraternal reunion remains distinct from formal constitutional restoration.
- Translator or editor paratext remains outside primary narrative evidence.
- Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence.
- Later interpretation remains deferred.
