## Documentary movement

1. Calpe is described as midway between Byzantium and Heraclea, naturally defensible, watered, timbered, fertile, harbor-equipped, and capable of holding ten thousand inhabitants.
2. The soldiers camp on the beach because they are averse to occupying a site easily convertible into a city; their stated horizon remains safe return to Hellas with gains.
3. Favorable sacrifice permits an expedition to seek provisions and bury the Arcadian dead. Bodies are buried where possible and a cenotaph is raised for the missing.
4. A general assembly makes renewed division punishable by death and restores the former generals. Cheirisophus has died and Neon has succeeded him.
5. Sacrifices for departure remain unfavorable. Suspicion that Xenophon is manipulating the seer for a colony leads him to invite public and expert inspection.
6. Repeated sacrifices for departure and then for provisioning remain adverse despite hunger, changed victims, and Cleanor's supervision.
7. Neon leads about two thousand volunteers to nearby villages. They scatter to forage and are attacked by Pharnabazus's cavalry and Bithynians; no fewer than five hundred are killed.
8. Xenophon sacrifices a wagon bullock, leads the available men under thirty, and recovers the surviving remnant.
9. At sunset Bithynians attack through the brush, kill pickets, and drive others into camp. The Greeks strengthen the outposts and keep armed watch all night.

## Preserved distinctions

- Natural suitability for settlement is not treated as political consent.
- The soldiers' motives and return to Hellas remain distinct from the site's objective advantages.
- Favorable sacrifice for burial remains distinct from adverse sacrifice for departure and provisioning.
- Public inspection establishes procedural openness, not the truth or cause of sacrificial signs.
- Accusation of colonization remains an attributed accusation, not narrator certification.
- Unified constitutional command remains distinct from Neon's separate operational action.
- Benevolent intent remains distinct from competent execution.
- Xenophon's refusal to lead before favorable signs remains distinct from his later rescue after a changed emergency and new sacrifice.
- Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence.
- Later comparison remains deferred.
