## Documentary movement

1. Five generals remain, but their destinations conflict: Seuthes, the Chersonese under Spartan protection, or Asia.
2. Delay dissolves the army as soldiers sell or give away arms and disperse into cities.
3. Anaxibius welcomes the breakup as useful to Pharnabazus; Aristarchus succeeds Cleander and sells no fewer than four hundred lingering Cyreians.
4. Pharnabazus transfers cooperation to the current governor. Anaxibius then reverses course and orders Xenophon to reunite the army and convey it to Asia.
5. Xenophon returns with a galley, letter of authority, and escort order. The soldiers receive him joyfully.
6. Seuthes renews his invitation through Medosades, but Xenophon initially refuses while the Spartan crossing mission remains operative.
7. Neon separates about eight hundred men at Perinthus.
8. Aristarchus arrives with two warships, forbids transport, displaces Anaxibius's order with his current office, and threatens to sink anyone crossing.
9. Warned that entry may lead to seizure or delivery to Pharnabazus, Xenophon turns back under the stated need to sacrifice.
10. The victims are favorable to approaching Seuthes. Xenophon, Polycrates, and trusted delegates travel sixty furlongs by night.
11. Xenophon and Seuthes specify expected service, pay, land, refuge, kinship, and territorial promises before witnesses and exchange pledges.

## Preserved distinctions

- Incompatible generalship plans are not silently harmonized into a single army policy.
- Gifts and private hopes may influence judgment without automatically proving corruption.
- Cleander, Aristarchus, and Anaxibius are not collapsed into one coherent Spartan intention.
- Anaxibius's written authorization remains distinct from Aristarchus's current coercive capacity.
- The reported arrest warning is not converted into a verified completed plot.
- Xenophon's first refusal of Seuthes is kept distinct from his later negotiation after the Asian route is blocked.
- Favorable sacrifice is narrated authorization, not demonstrated divine causation or proof of Seuthes's trustworthiness.
- Negotiation by trusted witnesses is distinct from private self-dealing and from army ratification.
- Conditional pledges are not treated as completed service, payment, refuge, kinship, or territorial transfer.
- Translated wording is not treated as unmediated Greek evidence; later comparison remains deferred.
