## Documentary movement

Heracleides meets the army at the Delta with the proceeds of the captured spoil. Seuthes selects three pairs of mules and directs Xenophon to receive them and distribute the other teams. Xenophon postpones his own share and assigns the mules to Timasion, Cleanor, and Phryniscus, with ox teams going to officers.

The pay due for a completed month is remitted only in the equivalent of twenty days. Heracleides says that this is all his trafficking produced. Xenophon rebukes him for failing to secure the full amount, even by borrowing or selling his own coat. The rebuke produces a political reversal: Heracleides fears losing Seuthes's friendship, begins calumniating Xenophon, and shifts both soldier anger and Seuthes's vexation toward the visible intermediary who continues demanding payment.

Seuthes had repeatedly spoken of giving Xenophon Bisanthe, Ganos, and Neontichos upon return to the coast. After Heracleides warns against giving fortified towns to a man backed by troops, those promises disappear from speech without formal revocation.

Heracleides attempts to continue the campaign through the other generals, promising them two months' full pay. Timasion refuses to campaign without Xenophon even for five months' pay; Phryniscus and Cleanor agree. Seuthes rebukes Heracleides for excluding Xenophon. Xenophon answers the later private invitation by bringing every general and officer, and continuation follows joint consent rather than a private compact.

The force reduces Salmydessus, where wreck salvage is territorially divided by pillars and where earlier competition among wreckers reportedly caused killing. Beds, boxes, written books, and mariners' goods are taken. Seuthes's army grows larger than the Hellenic army through Odrysian and submitted-tribe accessions. Above Selybria, however, further pay remains absent, soldiers are deeply hostile to Xenophon, Seuthes is less friendly, and access to him is repeatedly obstructed.

## Safeguards

- Spoil proceeds are not equated with a transparent account.
- Twenty days' pay is not represented as full monthly payment.
- Xenophon's refusal of a present is documentary evidence against immediate self-enrichment, not certification of pure motive.
- Reported motives belonging to Heracleides and Seuthes remain narrator reports.
- Disappearing promises are not treated as formally revoked contracts.
- The generals' loyalty is not converted into proof of Xenophon's perfect leadership.
- Salmydessian wrecking practice is not certified as law or justice.
