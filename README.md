# Xenophon

This repository is a documentary and interpretive research environment for the independent reconstruction of Xenophon.

Its purpose is not to collect summaries about Xenophon. Its purpose is to preserve the textual conditions under which Xenophon's works can be read carefully: work identity, editions and translations, title and opening, narrative person, speakers and audiences, speeches and deeds, sequence, omissions, repetitions, contradictions, historical setting, and unresolved questions.

## Governing source rule

Xenophon's surviving works are the primary authorities for reconstructing Xenophon. Later interpreters may guide questions, identify textual features, and propose readings, but they may not replace Xenophon's texts or compel those texts to confirm an interpretation.

The repository begins with one registered secondary witness: Leo Strauss's essay **"Xenophon's Anabasis"**, printed in *Studies in Platonic Political Philosophy* (University of Chicago Press, 1983), pp. 105-136. The essay was left by Strauss in handwritten form, transcribed after his death, and first published in 1975. It is therefore a qualified secondary interpretive source for this repository, not a substitute for an independently reviewed witness of Xenophon's *Anabasis*.

## Repository safeguards

1. Reconstruct each Xenophontic work independently before cross-work synthesis.
2. Preserve edition, translation, manuscript, editorial, and locator uncertainty.
3. Distinguish Xenophon the author, the narrator, Xenophon as a character, quoted speakers, reported opinion, and later interpreters.
4. Read speech together with deed, audience, occasion, sequence, and outcome.
5. Treat titles, openings, endings, ordering, silence, repetition, and changes of narrative person as evidence without assuming in advance that every pattern is deliberate.
6. Distinguish practical success, effective authority, legitimate authority, justice, piety, and wisdom.
7. Do not treat victory, office, wealth, popularity, sacrifice, omen, oracle, or later reputation as self-interpreting proof.
8. Preserve uncertainty and rival explanations where the evidence does not decide.
9. Never silently revise completed records; use additive, versioned correction and migration.
10. Keep secondary interpretation subordinate to primary-text reconstruction.

## Initial production sequence

1. Establish the repository manifest, source hierarchy, reading protocol, and corpus registry.
2. Register the reviewed Strauss witness and reconstruct its argument without adopting it as repository doctrine.
3. Admit one or more independently reviewed primary witnesses of Xenophon's *Anabasis*.
4. Conduct a book-by-book reconstruction of the primary text.
5. Compare the independent primary reconstruction with Strauss's interpretation only after the primary reading is complete.
6. Expand to Xenophon's other works without forcing them into a prior synthesis.

## Initial structure

- `manifest.yaml` - machine-readable repository entry point
- `method/` - source hierarchy and reading discipline
- `corpus/` - registered source and witness index
- `studies/` - source-specific reconstructions
- `audits/` - completion, limitations, and next required work
- `history/` - preserved founding and migration records

The repository is open research. Operational structure does not certify a final interpretation of Xenophon.
